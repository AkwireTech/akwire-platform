High Availability & Redundancy

Learn how enterprise networks remain available through redundant components, fault tolerance, load balancing, clustering, and elimination of single points of failure.

NETWORK RESILIENCE

High Availability

Enterprise networks are designed to remain operational even when hardware, software, or communication links fail. High availability minimizes downtime and improves reliability.

Maximum Uptime Keep critical services online.
Fault Tolerance Continue operating after failures.
RESILIENCE

High Availability vs Fault Tolerance

High Availability

Uses redundancy and rapid recovery methods to minimize downtime and maintain service availability.

Fault Tolerance

Allows systems to continue operating immediately even when a hardware component fails.

TRAFFIC DISTRIBUTION

Load Balancing

Load balancers distribute client requests across multiple servers, improving performance, availability, and scalability while preventing individual systems from becoming overloaded.

Spread traffic across multiple resources instead of relying on one server.
REDUNDANCY

Clustering

Clusters combine multiple servers so workloads continue running even if one server becomes unavailable.

Cluster members support each other during failures.
LINK REDUNDANCY

NIC Teaming & Link Aggregation

NIC teaming combines multiple network interfaces into a single logical connection to increase bandwidth and provide automatic failover if one network adapter or cable fails.

Multiple network connections improve both performance and availability.
HARDWARE RESILIENCE

RAID & Redundant Power

RAID protects storage from disk failures, while redundant power supplies ensure that equipment continues operating if one power supply fails. These technologies reduce single points of failure.

Duplicate critical hardware whenever continuous operation is required.
