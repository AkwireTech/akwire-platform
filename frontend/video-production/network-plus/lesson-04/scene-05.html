Disaster Recovery & Business Continuity

Learn how organizations prepare for disasters, maintain critical operations, recover network services quickly, and minimize business disruption.

NETWORK RESILIENCE

Preparing for Disruptions

Hardware failures, cyberattacks, natural disasters, and human error can interrupt business operations. Disaster recovery planning prepares organizations to restore services quickly and safely.

Service Availability Restore critical systems rapidly.
Preparedness Plan before disasters occur.
PLANNING

Disaster Recovery vs Business Continuity

Disaster Recovery (DR)

Focuses on restoring IT systems, infrastructure, applications, and network services after an outage.

Business Continuity (BC)

Ensures critical business operations continue during and after a disruptive event.

RECOVERY OBJECTIVES

RTO vs RPO

RTO

Recovery Time Objective defines the maximum acceptable downtime before services must be restored.

RPO

Recovery Point Objective defines the maximum acceptable amount of data loss after an incident.

DATA PROTECTION

Backup Strategies

Reliable disaster recovery depends on regular backups, secure storage, off-site copies, cloud replication, and periodic recovery testing.

A backup is valuable only if it can be successfully restored.
HIGH AVAILABILITY

Redundant Infrastructure

Redundant links, backup power, duplicate servers, clustered storage, and secondary internet connections help eliminate single points of failure.

Redundancy increases availability even when hardware fails.
VALIDATION

Disaster Recovery Testing

Recovery plans should be tested regularly through tabletop exercises, simulated failures, and full recovery drills to verify that procedures actually work.

An untested recovery plan cannot be trusted during an emergency.
