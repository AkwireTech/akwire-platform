CIA Triad & Core Security Principles

Understand the three foundational objectives of information security: confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

SECURITY FOUNDATION

What Are We Protecting?

Information security is concerned with protecting information and systems from unauthorized access, improper modification, and disruption of service. The CIA triad provides a foundational framework for understanding these goals.

Protect Information Keep information secure throughout its lifecycle.
Security Objectives Confidentiality, integrity, and availability.
OBJECTIVE 1.2

The CIA Triad

Confidentiality

Information is accessible only to authorized individuals, systems, or processes.

EXAMPLE Access controls & encryption
Integrity

Information remains accurate, complete, and protected from unauthorized modification.

EXAMPLE Hashing & digital signatures
Availability

Authorized users can access information and systems when needed.

EXAMPLE Redundancy & fault tolerance
CORE PRINCIPLES

Beyond the CIA Triad

Security+ also identifies additional concepts that support secure systems, including authentication, authorization, accounting, and non-repudiation.

Security requires more than protecting data. Organizations must also verify identity, control access, and maintain accountability.

MODERN SECURITY

Zero Trust

Zero Trust removes implicit trust and requires access decisions to be continuously evaluated according to identity, policy, and security context.

Never automatically trust a user or system simply because it is inside the network.
