Data Ownership & Governance

Data protection also depends on clearly defined ownership, responsibilities, retention requirements, and policies that control how information is managed.

LESSON 04 • DATA GOVERNANCE

Data needs clear ownership and accountability

Organizations need defined roles and policies to decide who is responsible for data, who may use it, how long it should be retained, and when it should be securely removed.

DATA ROLES Different roles support responsible data management
Data Owner Responsible for determining how data should be classified and protected.
Data Custodian Manages and protects data on behalf of the data owner.
Data Processor Processes information according to defined requirements and agreements.
Data Controller Determines purposes and methods for processing certain personal information.
DATA GOVERNANCE Establish responsibility throughout the data lifecycle
Assign Establish responsible data roles.
Define Establish policies and requirements.
Monitor Verify compliance and proper handling.
Improve Update controls when requirements change.
DATA RETENTION Keep information only as long as required
REQUIREMENT Retention Period
STORAGE Retain
END OF LIFE Dispose
Retention should be based on legitimate business, legal, regulatory, and operational requirements.
GOVERNANCE RISK Poor governance can create unnecessary exposure
WEAK GOVERNANCE Uncontrolled Data Unclear ownership, excessive access, missing policies, and unnecessary retention increase risk.
VS
STRONG GOVERNANCE Managed Data Defined ownership, clear policies, controlled access, monitoring, and secure disposal improve accountability.
SECURITY+ PRACTICE Four questions for data governance scenarios
Who owns it? Identify the person or role responsible for the data.
Who manages it? Determine who implements and maintains protections.
How long? Determine the appropriate retention requirement.
What happens next? Follow the required archival or disposal process.
EXAM MEMORY

Owner → Custodian → Policy → Retention → Disposal

When a Security+ scenario asks about data responsibility, identify who owns the information, who manages it, which policies apply, how long it must be retained, and what happens at the end of its lifecycle.

LESSON 04 • SCENE 28 SUMMARY Governance makes data protection accountable
Data ownership establishes responsibility for classification and protection.
Custodians and other authorized roles help implement data protections.
Retention requirements determine how long information should be maintained.
Data should be securely disposed of when retention requirements end.
