Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery

Business continuity keeps critical business activities operating during disruption, while disaster recovery focuses on restoring affected technology, systems, and data.

ONE DISRUPTION. TWO RELATED OBJECTIVES.

Keep the business operating and restore what failed.

A major outage can affect employees, customers, facilities, applications, and data. Continuity and recovery planning coordinate the response so essential services can continue and disrupted technology can be restored.

UNDERSTAND THE DISTINCTION Business continuity versus disaster recovery
BUSINESS CONTINUITY

Keep essential operations going

  • Maintain critical business services
  • Coordinate people and alternate processes
  • Reduce interruption to customers
  • Use workarounds or alternate locations
DISASTER RECOVERY

Restore technology and data

  • Recover servers and applications
  • Restore data from backups
  • Recover networks and infrastructure
  • Validate systems before returning to service
The plans support each other: technology recovery helps business services return to normal, while continuity procedures can keep critical work moving during restoration.
COORDINATED RESPONSE Move from disruption toward normal operations
01 Disruption An outage affects critical services or resources.
02 Maintain Activate continuity procedures and workarounds.
03 Recover Restore technology, applications, and data.
04 Return Validate services and resume normal operations.
BUSINESS IMPACT ANALYSIS Decide what must recover first
STEP 01 Identify Functions List the organization's essential activities.
STEP 02 Assess Impact Understand financial, operational, and customer effects.
STEP 03 Set Priorities Determine which services require earlier recovery.
STEP 04 Assign Resources Identify people, systems, facilities, and dependencies.
Business impact analysis helps establish recovery priorities and informs recovery objectives. Criticality and dependencies influence the order of restoration.
MEASURE RECOVERY REQUIREMENTS Understand RTO and RPO
RECOVERY TIME OBJECTIVE RTO

The target time within which a service or system should be restored after disruption.

EXAMPLE Restore within 4 hours
RECOVERY POINT OBJECTIVE RPO

The maximum acceptable amount of data loss measured in time before the disruption.

EXAMPLE Lose no more than 1 hour of data
RTO measures time to restore service. RPO measures how far back in time recovered data may need to go. They are different recovery requirements.
COORDINATION AND READINESS Make the plans work together
Clear Roles Define decision-makers, response teams, and escalation paths.
Shared Resources Confirm access to backups, alternate facilities, and equipment.
Communication Coordinate employee, leadership, vendor, and customer updates.
Joint Testing Exercise continuity and technology recovery dependencies together.
A recovered application may still be unusable if staff, network connections, facilities, or supporting services are not ready.
EXAM MEMORY

Continuity keeps work moving. Recovery restores technology.

Business impact analysis helps determine priorities. RTO defines the target recovery time, while RPO defines the acceptable data-loss window. Coordinated plans help the organization respond and recover as one operation.
