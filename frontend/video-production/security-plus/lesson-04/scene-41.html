Disaster Recovery Plan Maintenance & Updates

Disaster recovery plans must remain accurate as an organization changes. Regular reviews, documented updates, clear responsibilities, and follow-up testing help ensure recovery procedures remain useful.

ACT 01 · THE BIG PICTURE
↻ DR PLAN
REVIEW UPDATE TEST

A recovery plan must keep pace with change.

New systems, staff changes, updated business priorities, and revised infrastructure can make old procedures unreliable.

A plan is only useful when it reflects the current environment.
ACT 02 · WHEN TO REVIEW

What triggers a plan update?

◷

Scheduled Reviews

Review the plan at defined intervals.

⌘

Technology Changes

Update new systems, networks, and dependencies.

◎

Personnel Changes

Verify contacts, roles, and assigned responsibilities.

↗

Business Changes

Account for new priorities, locations, and services.

! Do not wait for a disaster to discover that information is outdated.

ACT 05 · ACCOUNTABILITY

Everyone needs a clear role.

◉

Plan Owner

Coordinates reviews and tracks required changes.

⌘

Technical Teams

Validate system details and recovery procedures.

◆

Leadership

Approves priorities, resources, and major changes.

↗

Response Teams

Confirm they can follow the updated instructions.

ACT 06 · VERIFY THE CHANGES

An updated plan must still work.

01

Review

Check whether the update addresses the change.

02

Exercise

Walk through the revised recovery steps.

03

Measure

Compare results with recovery objectives.

04

Document

Record findings, assign fixes, and retest.

! An update is not complete until the affected procedures have been reviewed and, when appropriate, tested.
ACT 07 · EXAM MEMORY
SEC+

Remember the maintenance cycle.

Review → Update → Approve → Test

If an exam question describes outdated contacts, changed infrastructure, or untested recovery steps, consider plan maintenance and validation.

ACT 08 · KEY TAKEAWAYS

Keep the plan current and usable.

✓

Review plans regularly and after significant changes.

✓

Verify contacts, systems, procedures, and priorities.

✓

Control versions, approvals, and distribution.

✓

Test revised procedures and document the results.

41
FINAL CONCEPT

Recovery readiness is an ongoing responsibility.

A disaster recovery plan should evolve with the organization. Regular maintenance, clear ownership, controlled updates, and testing help keep recovery procedures dependable.
