High Availability & Fault Tolerance

ACT 01 • AVAILABILITY

Critical services should remain available

High availability reduces service interruptions by using redundancy, resilient architecture, and recovery mechanisms that keep critical systems operating when individual components fail.

ACT 02 • AVAILABILITY CONCEPTS

Availability depends on multiple protective strategies

Redundancy Additional components provide backup capacity
Failover Services move to an alternate system
Load Balancing Traffic is distributed across resources
Fault Tolerance Systems continue operating after component failure
ACT 03 • RESILIENT ARCHITECTURE

Build resilience across the service path

01
Detect Identify a component failure
02
Fail Over Switch to an alternate resource
03
Continue Maintain essential service
04
Restore Repair the failed component
ACT 04 • RESILIENCE STRATEGIES

Different technologies support availability

Clustering Multiple systems work together to provide continued service.
Load Balancing Workloads are distributed across available resources.
Redundant Storage Data remains available when a storage component fails.
Redundancy is most effective when failure of one component does not become a single point of failure.
ACT 05 • SINGLE POINT OF FAILURE

Resilience depends on eliminating critical weak points

Single Point of Failure One failed component can interrupt the entire service because no effective alternate path exists.
Redundant Architecture Alternate components or paths allow services to continue when one resource fails.
ACT 06 • PRACTICE

Security+ availability reminders

01 Identify critical services
02 Remove single points of failure
03 Use redundancy and failover
04 Test resilience before failure occurs
EXAM MEMORY

Redundancy reduces the impact of failure

High availability uses resilient designs so a single component failure does not automatically cause a service outage.

SCENE SUMMARY

What you should remember

High availability helps critical services remain operational.
Redundancy provides alternate resources when components fail.
Failover moves services to an available resource.
Single points of failure should be identified and reduced.
FINAL CONCEPT

Design for failure instead of assuming it will not happen

High availability and fault-tolerant designs prepare systems to continue operating when individual components fail.
