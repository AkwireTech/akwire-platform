Data Breaches & Incident Response

ACT 01 • DATA BREACHES

A data breach can expose sensitive information

A data breach occurs when unauthorized individuals gain access to protected information. Organizations need an incident response process to contain the breach, investigate what happened, and reduce further damage.

ACT 02 • COMMON BREACH SOURCES

Breaches can happen in different ways

Credential Compromise Stolen or exposed credentials
Malware Malicious software accesses data
Insider Activity Authorized users misuse access
Misconfiguration Security controls are incorrectly configured
ACT 03 • INCIDENT RESPONSE

Respond in a controlled sequence

01
Identify Detect and confirm the incident
02
Contain Limit the impact
03
Eradicate Remove the threat
04
Recover Restore secure operations
ACT 04 • EVIDENCE & DOCUMENTATION

Preserve evidence while controlling the incident

Collect Evidence Preserve logs, systems, and relevant records
Document Actions Record findings, decisions, and response actions
Analyze the Incident Determine the scope, cause, and impact
Preserve evidence before making unnecessary changes to affected systems.
ACT 05 • RESPONSE MATTERS

Poor response can increase the damage

Uncontrolled Response Evidence may be lost, systems may be changed, and the full scope of the incident may remain unknown.
Structured Response Teams contain the threat, preserve evidence, restore systems, and document lessons learned.
ACT 06 • PRACTICE

Security+ response reminders

01 Confirm the incident
02 Contain the affected systems
03 Preserve evidence and document actions
04 Recover and review
EXAM MEMORY

Identify → Contain → Eradicate → Recover

Remember the incident response sequence and the importance of preserving evidence.

SCENE SUMMARY

What you should remember

Data breaches can come from credentials, malware, insiders, or misconfiguration.
Incident response should follow a controlled process.
Evidence should be preserved and response actions documented.
Recovery should be followed by review and lessons learned.
FINAL CONCEPT

A breach is an event. Response is a process.

Effective incident response limits damage, protects evidence, restores secure operations, and helps the organization improve after the incident.
